About

What I work on, and why

I'm a final-year Computer Science and Engineering undergraduate at United International University, working across full-stack platforms and applied ML research. Most of what I build sits at the intersection of the two: systems that use speech, text, and multimodal models to make a judgment — a skill score, a diagnostic signal, a recommendation — and are built so that judgment can be inspected, not just trusted. That's shown up as an AI skill-assessment platform with a full audio evaluation pipeline, a hypergraph model for catching drug-interaction risk that standard pairwise methods miss, and a concept-bottleneck architecture for depression screening that explains its reasoning in human-readable terms rather than a black-box score. I care about the same thing in both the engineering and the research: building something that works, and being able to say precisely why it works.

Data Mining — Higher-Order Drug Interaction Detection

CASH-HyperLBS

Data Mining Course Project
Research question

Resolving Sign Ambiguity in Higher-Order Drug Interactions via Calibrated Hypergraph Learning — pairwise interaction models miss adverse events that only emerge from three or more drugs interacting together, and even when a higher-order signal is found, its direction (protective vs. harmful) is often ambiguous.

Method
  1. 01 Co-designed a signed-ambiguous hypergraph framework using HODDI/FAERS data and outcome-independent mechanistic features from CYP450, Reactome, ChEMBL, ATC, and TWOSIDES
  2. 02 Specified a modular PyTorch Geometric pipeline: SapBERT drug harmonization, three-channel message passing, Monte Carlo structural-frustration estimation, contrastive pretraining, confidence-weighted LLM distillation
  3. 03 Defined a reproducible prospective evaluation using a strict temporal split with AUROC, AUPRC, and precision–recall analysis
Contribution

Co-designed the signed hypergraph formulation and the evaluation protocol, without claiming performance results beyond what was reported.

Tools
Python PyTorch PyTorch Geometric SapBERT Llama-3

Course project — specified and reproducibly evaluated

Final Year Design Project — Neuro-Symbolic Multimodal AI

Interpretable Depression Screening

Jan 2026 — Jun 2026
Research question

Depression screening models are usually black boxes — a PHQ-8 severity score with no visibility into what drove it. Can a concept-bottleneck architecture produce the same estimate while staying interpretable, and flag proxy anxiety and distress indicators along the way?

Method
  1. 01 Five-member FYDP fusing Wav2Vec2 audio and MentalRoBERTa text through a 15-concept bottleneck
  2. 02 Built an LLM concept-annotation pipeline across 275 E-DAIC transcripts and 15 concepts, producing 4,125 structured annotations
  3. 03 Pretrained MentalRoBERTa on 27,977 four-class mental-health posts and co-trained the concept-bottleneck configurations
  4. 04 Ran ablation studies and λ ± 0.1 sensitivity analysis
Contribution

Built the concept-annotation pipeline and contributed to the co-training and evaluation of the bottleneck configurations as part of Team Reboot.

4.03 MAE
0.64 Macro-F1
0.69 PR-AUC

Bioinformatics — Copy Number Variant Detection

PScnv-PoNMatch

Bioinformatics Course Paper, Jun 2026
Research question

Standard CNV detection tools assume matched normal samples, which frequently aren't available in targeted sequencing — how much detection accuracy can be recovered with adaptive panel-of-normals selection and purity-aware calling instead?

Method
  1. 01 Co-authored a framework combining Panel-of-Normals normalization, IQR-guarded diploid-anchor selection, Ridge denoising, cellularity-adaptive Z-segmentation, and purity-aware calling
  2. 02 Benchmarked against XHMM, CNVkit, CODEX2, and standard PScnv on 75 simulated multi-cancer profiles across five tumor-purity levels and 122 sparse target regions
Contribution

Co-authored the method and ran the benchmark comparison against four baseline tools.

0.7188 Precision
0.8169 Recall
0.7314 F1
+42.9% F1 vs. standard PScnv

Independent Research — Explainable Multi-Task Learning

Multimodal Mental Health Prediction

Jan 2026 — Present
Research question

Can one multi-task, multimodal model jointly predict depression (PHQ-8), PTSD (PCL-C), and weakly-supervised anxiety from interview text and audio — and explain, token by token, which parts of the input drove each prediction?

Method
  1. 01 Transformer text encoders and speech representations fused via cross-attention and gated late fusion
  2. 02 Modality dropout for reliable inference under missing-modality conditions; uncertainty-weighted multi-task objectives
  3. 03 Token/time-window attribution via Integrated Gradients, validated with faithfulness testing (masking and deletion curve analysis)
  4. 04 Evaluation protocol: MAE, RMSE, F1, AUROC, ablation, and calibration
Contribution

Designed the fusion architecture and the explainability evaluation protocol; preparing an IEEE-format manuscript.

Tools
PyTorch Transformers wav2vec2 / HuBERT Integrated Gradients

Manuscript in preparation (IEEE format)
